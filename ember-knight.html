LIVE CAPTUREROOM 14 — THE LOW TOWER

Three cinders, one kingdom

The world splits into three failing regions, each keeping its own kind of light alive. You choose the order you save them in — and each choice changes who's still standing by the end.

1

The Hollow Wood

A forest that grows in reverse. Every step back in, its trees get a season younger — and the wildlife remembers.

2

The Cinder Caves

Underground rivers of light instead of water. Wade in too far and the caves start to remember you differently.

3

The Sky Bastion

The last fortress, built high enough to keep its own weather. It has not let anyone leave in eleven years.

Who's still lighting the way

A small, original cast — nobody borrowed, nobody licensed. Everyone here answers to nobody but the story.

Protagonist

Rue

Former lamplighter, current arsonist of last resort. Carries the coal in a cracked lantern, one hand always over the glass.

Companion

Cinder

A moth made of loose flame. Won't fly in a straight line, won't explain why, always right about which door not to open.

Warden of the Bastion

Aurelac

Sealed the last fortress eleven years ago to keep something out. Has not yet said what, or whether it worked.

Three things guarding their dark

No boss is a damage sponge. Each one changes the rules of its room the moment the fight starts.

The Hollow Wood

The Root-That-Remembers

Grows back whatever you cut. You're not meant to kill it — you're meant to convince it the season changed.

The Cinder Caves

The Long Wyrm

You never see all of it at once. The fight is a sequence of rooms, and it's already in the next one.

The Sky Bastion

Warden Aurelac

Not a monster — a person who has been right about a threat for eleven straight years, and it shows.

Every room, lit by hand

No dynamic global lighting — each screen has its own light sources placed and tuned individually, the way it was done when memory was measured in kilobytes.

A score played on real hardware

Every track is composed on a chiptune tracker, then run back through a tape hiss pass so it sounds like it's coming out of an old speaker, not a plugin. Click a row to preview.
